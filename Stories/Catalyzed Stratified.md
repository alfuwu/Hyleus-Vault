A nightshift worker sat hunched before several rudimentary tabloids that displayed text via magically emitted light, the glare illuminating her face in a ghastly light. She was one of several dozen personnel tracking any and all types of known magical anomalies across the continent. Something about "national security" and "duty to the people".

She didn't care about the reasons, though. The pay was generous for the amount of effort needed, and that was all that mattered. The dozens of contracts she'd been forced to sign before actually beginning the job all those years ago could go fuck right off. During her employment, she'd only ever spotted two anomalies; one just outside Ckretion, and another inside the Atta Jangutta. Only the anomaly near Ckretion was investigated; the Atta Jangutta was the source of a plentitude of anomalies, and attempting to research each one of them would be suicide for any intrepid scientist. Both anomalies were rather minor, with the one near Ckretion turning out to be a psychic necromancer attempting to create a localized Psychic Leyline connecting him to his undead thralls. That man had been summarily arrested and executed. Good riddance.

Strange, then, it was, when all the monitors in front of her crashed. Strange indeed. Perhaps slightly worrying, one could even venture to say. It had never happened once during all her years working her job. Perhaps the artifice merely failed due to age, but if it wasn't age that fried it...

The woman hastily stood up from her chair, almost knocking her half-empty mug of ackja off the desk with a clumsy hand. She began moving in an odd half-walking, half-running manner, unsure whether or not her equipment suddenly shutting down was a situation that warranted a breach of etiquette. She, personally, was leaning towards the latter of the two, but one could never know for certain.

Corridor after corridor she turned down, muttering under her breath curses at whichever incompetent architect designed such a labyrinthine building.

She swiftly arrived at her superior's office, and, after two nigh-frantic knocks, a pleasantly baritone voice echoed out from beyond the door. "Come in."

The woman swung the door inwards and shot the man a salute that could *maybe* pass if one was squinting hard enough. The man could see the urgency in her gaze and posture, and wisely decided that such minor things were not the most pressing topic.

"At ease," he began, waiting for the woman to lower her salute before continuing on in a demanding tone. "Report."

"Sir! The equipment broke suddenly. I am unsure, but think this might indicate a Class Vec anomaly."

"Class Vec?" the man asked, a masterfully raised eyebrow painting a look of skepticism upon his face that would make an artist weep. "I doubt it. I'll get some artificers to fix the equipment and find out what went wrong. You are dismissed."

"Sir! Is that it? What about the anomaly? Aren't you going to investigate it?"

"What *about* it? If a Class Vec anomaly has truly emerged, there is nothing we can do but pray it never comes near us. And until the equipment's fixed, we can't get any information about this potential anomaly's whereabouts."

Suddenly feeling a little frustrated and more than a bit useless, the woman exited the room. The man rubbed his temples for a second, flagging down a courier in the hallway to tell the artificers to fix the equipment in room 339 before returning to the paperwork he was doing before the intrusion as if nothing had happened.

*Ckretion's capital, Ackalus*


The loaves of bread steamed in the fire-hot oven, the smell gradually wafting out like an omen of happy days and good times. The charismatic young man working the bakery late in the evening enjoyed the tranquility and stillness that came in the later hours of the day, savoring the cozy quiet stemming from the fact that only a few customers loitered around at this late a hour.

His name was Fellucko Mec Yridis, the Yridis bit meaning "stalwart" in Ckretion.

The bell outside the bakery rung once, twice, thrice, and then a few more times after that, indicating a customer entering the building. Fellucko looked up and broke into a smile upon seeing who it was. A girl stood in the entryway, her long golden hair damp and her blazer wet from the rain pattering outside. She had clearly come over in a rush; her hair stuck out in all manner of directions, licked by the wind, and was still panting.

"Icko! What're you doing here? Don't you have Academy work to be doing?"

The troubled silence and worried look Icko wore wiped the grin clean off Fellucko's face. "What's wrong?"

"They're evacuating the Academy out of the capital, Felli," she spoke, still slightly breathless.

That made Fellucko pause. "They're evacuating? What happened? What's going on?"

"I don't know either!" Icko exclaimed in frustration. "But we have to go. Right now."

"Just like that?" Fellucko asked, looking around at his bakery. What would happen to it if he left it all alone? Burglars, surely. Assuming whatever they were evacuating from wasn't even worse.

"Just like that," Icko confirmed with almost frantic pleading.

"Alright. Sure. Fine," Fellucko said tiredly, grabbing a couple loaves of bread and stuffing them into his satchel.

They exited the bakery together, the dim candlelight inside snuffed out and the oven turned off. Fellucko flipped the sign in front of it to the side that said "CLOSED" in large letters, hoping that that would be enough to deter any criminal-hopefuls.

They trudged through the rain along the cobblestone pathways, their shoes making small splashes in the puddles as the rain drenched both of them in frigid embrace.

"Do you think they'll tell us what this whole thing is about once we're out?" Fellucko asked, more to make small talk than anything.

"They better," Icko muttered. They continued in silence after that, the normally chatty Fellucko at a loss for what to talk about. The only times the Academy had ever evacuated before were during states of national emergency; enemy armies marching on the capital, stuff like that. So why were they evacuating this time? Fellucko simply couldn't get the question off his mind.

Ckretion hadn't been at war with another nation for decades. They were at the strongest they'd ever been, both in magical potency and intellectual aptitude. Things that were luxuries in other nations, such as literacy, were commonplace in Ckretion.

Perhaps it was a rogue archmage? An demon, even? The Academy shouldn't have any issues handling a single archmage, though; several archmagi took up residence within the Academy, not to mention the fact that the Ckretion military had several archmagi itself.

So was it a demon? No, if it were, where was all the destruction? Wouldn't a demon powerful and foolish enough to threaten the Academy be wreaking havoc? The late evening was sleepy; dreary even.

Quiet.

A chill went down Fellucko's spine. It was too quiet. Much much much too quiet. Where was all the noise? There were no leaves rustling, no pebbles skipping. Even the sound of his own footsteps was hard to make out. He quickly glanced at Icko, and, to his relief, she was still there. Still walking alongside him.

"Icko?"

"Mm?"

"Isn't it... too quiet?"

Icko paused for a second. She looked up, and for a split second, Fellucko saw an expression of terror so pure, so untainted, it startled him.

"Felli! We need to run! Now! C'mon!" Icko began sprinting down the street as fast as she could, taking Fellucko by surprise. He sputtered for a moment or two before running after her.

The sky above began cracking. It started small, just a tiny pinprick of absolute darkness promising oblivion to all who neared. But it grew. And it grew *beautifully.*

Tendrils of black began spreading through the sky like a virus, slowly at first but picking up speed rapidly. Fellucko saw a small flash of light form in Icko's hands, indicative of a spell matrix forming, and suddenly felt his physical exhaustion vanish. A light flashed again in her hands, presumably for herself, this time.

They ran like that for another ten minutes before they saw the first... *thing* from the sky. It was black, just like the cracks in the sky. It loosely resembled a human, perhaps closer to a tarhan. It was hunched over almost ninety degrees with just a stub for a head. Its arms were long, longer than its torso and legs combined, and ended in indistinguishable mist. It felt as though it made negative noise; it's landing was silent, despite shock visibly traveling up its limbs. 

Fellucko let out a strangled yelp, doubling down on his efforts to run away. Try as he might, though, the capital city was large. Extremely so. And the weird void in the sky showed precisely zero signs of slowing down.

Icko formed a simple *Creation* spell matrix, targeted at the monster itself. Surprisingly, the *Creation* spell, which was not offensive in any capacity, caused the creature to shriek. It clawed at itself, tearing off chunks of nothing, before it exploded in a big cloud of nothing.

Fellucko turned his gaze back to the road ahead.

That's when the city shook. Buildings vibrated; some even collapsed. Wails of fear, anguish, and despair could be heard from all around now; a stark contrast to the tranquil just half an hour earlier.

Fellucko kept running. He and Icko were making steady progress — if they kept it up, they might make it out without a scratch. He estimated they were probably halfway there.

And then the sky died.

Black tears wept from above, tainting the world and burning life away with nary a touch. Icko quickly erected a shield above the both of them, yet the drops of hell started eating away at the mana construct voraciously.

"Over there!" Icko shouted, pointing desperately at a small house with its door left slightly ajar. The owners obviously left in a hurry, and Fellucko didn't have to wonder why.
