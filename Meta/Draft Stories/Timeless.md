Time.

It was a funny thing, really. Ever encroaching, ever marching onwards to some distant, abstract land called the "future".

It was one of the least tampered-with rules of reality. Off the top of his head, Emmanuel could only think of three examples.

Btrielix, the deity of Time, the Atta Jangutta, and Fel of the Abyss.

Technically, the amount was much more. After all, many complex spell matrices and rituals required timing so precise that it was almost necessary for the caster's perception of time to be slowed. This could be classified as "tampering" with time. However, considering gravitation and relativistic speeds can achieve a somewhat similar slowing of one's perception of time, Emmanuel didn't really count it as a real tampering of time.

The three mentioned were the only things that could *revert* time.

Of the three, the Atta Jangutta stood out the most. It was not a person, after all, but rather a fixed monument. It was said to have formed some time in the future, its existence cascading down the river of time, an aberration on the otherwise unblemished tapestry of spacetime.

Others said it was formed during a game of poker between Btrielix and Fel of the Abyss, the fabric of time being warped so much by their constant reversals of its flow that it created a permanent scar upon the land. Coincidentally, these same people had never attended a higher educational institute than highschool.

In actuality, nobody, as far as Emmanuel could tell, truly knew how the Atta Jangutta came to be. So too did nobody know why Btrielix did not excise the cancer upon his Domain.

Many were grateful for the Atta Jangutta's existence, however. Such an intense warping of time allowed researchers over the millennia to better grasp an intuitive understanding of how time functioned.
