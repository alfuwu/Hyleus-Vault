Classical spellcasting is a broad school of magic that encompasses four distinct disciplines: rituals, spell matrices, artifice (archaically referred to as enchantment), and alchemy.

These disciplines are the first types of extrinsic magic to be discovered and recorded by the sapient races, hence their name as "classical".

Rituals involve using magical materials in a formation wherein the magics present within each material are drawn out and combined to create a predeterminate effect. Rituals function by connecting each of its constituent parts via a medium, such as blood, water, or any other liquid, depending on the ritual. This medium connects to each point in the ritual, which is typically laid out in a pentagram formation, and incites a reaction within the magical components of the ritual. The effect of a ritual is often single-use, but very powerful compared to creating and utilizing a spell matrix. Ancient ritualists have been known to create permanent rituals, though the process is highly inefficient when compared to what artifice can accomplish, while also being much less compact and portable.

Spell matrices are structured constructs of mana designed to influence the world by producing magical effects (such as conjuring fire or reshaping terrain). These matrices function using glyphs to program the mana's inherent nature in order to achieve a desired outcome. Spell matrixes are much like on-the-fly enchanted items, however glyphs and runes are non-interchangeable and will have unpredictable side-effects if they are assumed to be one and the same when performing artifice or crafting a spell matrix.

Artifice is the art of engraving runes onto objects. Objects created via artifice are typically referred to as either "enchanted" or as an "artifact", with the latter generally implying the object is of great power. The runes of an enchantment are always active so long as they are supplied with mana, which certain patterns of runes can draw in to create a self-sustaining enchanted item. Artifice generally requires less magical material than ritual magic, instead requiring vast technical knowledge of the magical runes and magic-imbued ink/metals.

Some notable magic-imbued metals are [[mithril]], a metal similar to titanium in mundane properties, and one of the few known mana-superconductors (metals capable of transferring mana with 100% efficiency), [[ksol]], a natural-forming metal that has not been observed to possess an upper bound to its mana storage capacity, and [[orichalcum]], a soft metal that is unusually resistant to mana and all forms of magic.

Lastly, alchemy. Alchemy shares many similarities with rituals, however, rather than using a medium to connect materials in a formation, alchemy requires active mana manipulation on the alchemist's part to fuse magical materials together within a solution. Alchemy often results in consumable items, rather than widescale environmental effects that rituals tend to produce. The most famous example of alchemy is the healing potion, invented by the ingenious [[Erwin Zvekk]] during the [[Industrial Era]]. An alchemical concoction that is bound by time (that is to say, has a temporary effect) is classified as a potion, while permanent alchemical concoctions, such as the legendary [[Elixir of Life]], are classified as elixirs. Elixirs are much more difficult to create than potions, naturally due to their nature of being permanent.

It is imperative to note that all four of these disciplines can and do have catastrophic consequences if performed incorrectly. A mistuned ritual might fracture ley-lines or render an area magically inert. A faulty spell matrix could collapse in on itself, triggering a mana feedback loop that consumes the caster's [[Soul]]. A misaligned artifact may implode, disintegrate, or even become a cursed object. And alchemical mishaps can range from explosive reactions to permanent bodily mutations. Precision, patience, and deep study are paramount—magic is not merely dangerous in amateur hands; it is lethal.

Classical spellcasting works on a macroscale rather than a microscale like the other, simpler laws of the world. The smallest unit recognized in classical spellcasting is the mana particle, which varies in size but has been measured at its smallest to be approximately 3 nanometers in diameter.

## Rituals

Rituals are another form of classical spellcasting, and one often associated with the occult. Rituals provide more powerful effects than spell matrices at a lesser cost, however they often require permanent sacrifices of valuable items, or, in some cases, living creatures.

## Spell Matrices

Spell matrices are an ancient but common form of magic in which one uses the auric mana surrounding their souls to construct a matrix of runes and pathways for mana to flow through. The runes act as modifiers to the mana you push through the matrix; shaping its nature and its goal, and the pathways allow linking one rune to another. This causes the mana to make certain, generally predictable, effects in an area or on an object. This is known as matrix-casting, or using spell matrices.

Runes in spell matrices are formed by the caster weaving their mana into certain shapes. How these shapes transform mana into tangible energy is unknown, though a widely researched topic.

There are more common runes and less common runes in spell matrices. Some of the most common runes are the conversion runes. These runes are simple; they tell the mana to transform itself into a different type of energy, such as kinetic, heat, electrical, etc. It is commonly used for creating simple spells, such as a small flame, or an orb of shining light. It is possible to use these conversion runes in more complex ways, by adding limiters and shaping runes. Essentially, the more mana that goes into a conversion rune, the more extreme the result, assuming the spell matrix doesn’t become overloaded and self-destruct. This means the more mana you put into a heat rune, the more heat it will output. It also means that when you put mana into a light rune, the visible light is entirely dependent on how much mana is put into the light rune. You can access the entire electromagnetic spectrum through the light rune, depending on how much mana you input, and thus it is possible to craft complex illusions by layering light conversion runes, diversion runes to separate mana flows, resistance runes to lower the strength of certain mana flows, etc.

Certain series and formations of runes create what is called a “harmonic series”. These are very volatile, yet powerful effects. The most famous of which is known as Derren’s Harmonic Series, which incites a chain reaction within the oxygen atoms present within the atmosphere to create a massive fireball. These harmonic series need just the right conditions to function properly; else they may have unintended side effects, not function, or otherwise be harmful to the user. This instability is the primary property of harmonic series keeping them from being explored as a path of spell matrix magic.

The above is what forms the unstable matrix—a matrix that, in theory, should work. However, reality has many differing factors that are not accounted for in theoretical scenarios, and as such the unstable matrix must be encased within a stabilizing matrix. This is where a significant portion of the difficulty of spell matrices becomes apparent—a magician must take into account many different factors and neutralize then for their spell to work as intended, else it might short-circuit or backfire.
