# The Fundamental Difference Between Miellochu and Magic
## Written by [[...]]
## Published 2331 [[Astana Calendar|ME]]

Magic, as we know it, has been used since the ancient times before we learned to start recording our history. Our knowledge of it has expanded greatly in the time since; yet the fact remains that the fundamental force was still used, even if not well understood. Recently, however, there has been great inventions completely forgoing the use of magic, in order to be widely applicable cheaply; an engine relying on steam as a fuel source rather than the ambient mana and a conductor to manage the engine's mana flows, to name one of the more notable examples. I foresee that travel may yet be revolutionized, if we continue down this path, and quick travel between cities may become a luxury of the past.

I dub this burgeoning new field of study *"miellochu"*; after the Miulyn root for complexity, *miell*; and the Miulyn suffix *"ochu"* to denote the pertaining to a study. The applications of this new field are vast indeed, and may bring an age of prosperity like no other we have seen before.

Now, we must discuss *why* miellochu is different from magic; what are its differences, and what exactly makes it so broadly applicable?

Well, to begin with, while magic is the art of bending reality to one's will, miellochu is the art of working *within* reality's confines. Miellochu utilizes our understanding of electricity, magnetism, light, matter, heat, and gravity, rather than magic's [[Classical Spellcasting|spell matrices]], [[Classical Spellcasting|rituals]], [[Classical Spellcasting|alchemy]], [[Classical Spellcasting|artifice]], [[vital magic]], and [[animancy]]. Use of these natural forces to achieve the desired result instead of simply bending reality until it cedes to your will is what makes miellochu *miellochu*.

This comes with one primary benefit: a trained [[soul]] is unneeded to manipulate miellochu. Technically speaking, there are artifacts that do not require a [[soul]] trained in the arcane to operate, but they are simultaneously few, monstrously complex, and incredibly costly.
