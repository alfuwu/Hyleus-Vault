The iolu

## Biology
Iolu are very similar to [[Human|humans]] in biology, and have been confirmed to have diverged from the homo sapiens genus at approximately 45,000 [[HUN Calendar|KE]].

All iolu are genetically predisposed towards [[vital magic]], going so far as requiring the magic to simply continue to live. This dependency emerged as an evolutionary trade-off—their metabolic processes evolved to extract lifeblood rather than solely relying on conventional digestion. An iolu can survive on regular food, but will gradually weaken and eventually enter a comatose state without periodic infusions of lifeblood. As the lifeblood of magically-inclined species is richer, iolu tend to want the blood of other sapients, who possess larger [[Soul|souls]] and thusly more [[mana]] to improve their magical capabilities.

Iolu eyes are quite different from [[human]] eyes, being adapted near-perfectly to total darkness, with pupils that dilate dramatically and a reflective tapetum lucidum layer (present in many nocturnal animals) that gives their eyes an distinctive gleam in low light. Their irises are capable of shifting colors, slightly modifying their perception, but, more importantly, being a trait considered "sexually attractive" by many of the species. This color-shifting appears to be linked to emotional states and [[mana]] fluctuations within their [[soul]], making iolu somewhat easier to read than other humanoids—though many have learned to consciously control this response. Their eyes are intensely photosensitive, with direct sunlight being capable of blinding them and permanently damaging their retinas.

As the iolu remain remarkably similar to [[Human|humans]], they remain sexually compatible with each other. After a couple of generations of interbreeding with [[Human|humans]], all traces of iolu biological differences will be gone from the offspring, though first-generation offspring between a [[human]] and an iolu still possess much of an iolu's biology. These half-iolu typically require less frequent lifeblood supplementation and generally possess moderately enhanced night vision, though not to the degree of full-blooded iolu.

## Culture
Iolu culture is, in many ways, utterly alien to the culture of the [[Human|humans]] in which the iolu developed alongside. Many cultural norms, especially those revolving around sexual reproduction, would be and have been met with scorn and contempt by other sapients in the past. For example, it is considered normal for a couple to consume the blood of the other, and to savor the taste. Most [[Human|humans]] would find the concept of eating their significant other, even if only a little, to be revolting.
